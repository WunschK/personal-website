Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) represents an exciting frontier in digital humanities, particularly for digital editions. In this post, I'll explore how this technology can enhance historical research.

## What is RAG?

RAG is a technique that combines large language models with external knowledge retrieval. Instead of relying solely on a model's training data, RAG systems can:

1. Query external databases or document collections
2. Retrieve relevant context
3. Generate informed responses based on that context

## Applications in Digital Editions

For digital editions of historical texts, RAG offers several compelling possibilities:

### Contextual Annotations
RAG systems can automatically suggest relevant annotations by retrieving information from:
- Historical dictionaries
- Contemporary documents
- Secondary literature
- Previous scholarly editions

### Cross-Reference Generation
When working with large corpora, RAG can identify:
- Parallel passages across documents
- Intertextual references
- Historical allusions
- Related correspondence

### Research Assistance
Scholars can ask natural language questions about:
- Specific historical events mentioned in texts
- Biographical information about persons
- Geographic context for places
- Temporal relationships between documents

## Challenges and Considerations

However, implementing RAG in digital humanities requires careful thought:

- **Accuracy**: LLMs can hallucinate facts, making verification crucial
- **Source Attribution**: Proper citation and transparency about sources
- **Interpretative Authority**: Balancing computational suggestions with scholarly judgment
- **Data Quality**: The retrieval component is only as good as its underlying knowledge base

## Moving Forward

The key is not to replace scholarly expertise but to augment it. RAG tools should be designed to:
- Make research more efficient
- Surface connections that might be overlooked
- Provide starting points for deeper investigation
- Always remain transparent about their sources and limitations
